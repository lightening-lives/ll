Ground studies · Lightening Lives

Nine grounds
for one darkfield.

The site opens on a blue-black at 11% lightness. Below are eight alternatives rendered against the real hero — same faces, same layer stack, same gold and green — so the ground is the only variable. Four are instrument colours; four are sampled from the company mark itself. The page has to convince labs, hospitals, government health authorities and NGOs at once, on hardware none of them chose.

What the numbers say
Hue is free. Every dark option holds ≥15:1 for body text, ≥10.3:1 for gold and green. Nothing in the accessibility budget forces the current blue-black — this is purely an aesthetic call.
What actually reads as "not black"
At 11% lightness every hue clips to near-black — petrol renders #000f14, forest #010f06. The lever is lightness, not hue: the ground has to reach roughly 16–20% before a colour declares itself at all.

The brand has no cool colour in it
Every hue in the mark falls between 29° and 133° — oxblood, sienna, gold, yellow, leaf. The site's ground sits at 250°, the opposite side of the wheel. That is a deliberate contrast, not an error — but it means nothing on the page currently shares the brand's temperature.
One token is genuinely off-brand
--color-probe is a mint green at hue 148; the mark's leaf is an olive at hue 133, 17 points darker. Gold and sienna match their brand counterparts almost exactly — the green is the only one that drifted.
Neither voice can own the page
Labs, hospitals, government health authorities and NGOs all carry equal weight, and they do not want the same thing. Specs alone lose the government and NGO evaluations; mission alone loses the lab one. So the ground stays clinical and quiet, and the brand's warmth lives in the accents, the people, the partner wall and the bone payoff — where all four audiences meet it.
The current gradient has 5 steps in it
From #02040b to #06090c across the whole page is 5 discrete 8-bit levels. On the 6-bit panels a district health office or an NGO field team is actually using, that either bands visibly or collapses to flat black in daylight. Lifting the ground to 16–20% roughly doubles the headroom.
